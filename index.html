Loggerhead Sea Turtle Conservation

This proof of concept has been built to demonstrate how conservation efforts could be visualised — where the bottlenecks are to successfully increasing the turtle population. The numbers are mainly placeholders not based on real data (the data may not even be available), but if someone would like to contact Lindsay MacVean on WhatsApp we could input real numbers or better estimates. The sliders at the bottom allow volunteers to see the effect of improving conservation on the baseline reproduction cycle.

Each slider is expressed relative to the baseline diagram on the left: it reduces that stage's baseline loss by the shown percentage. 0% = identical to the baseline; 100% = that loss fully eliminated. Losses without a slider (beach predators, aging, fertility) stay at their baseline rate. Every spoke's width equals the number of turtles, so both diagrams stay mass-conserving — note that improving an early stage (e.g. egg protection) lets more turtles reach later stages, so the counts lost to later threats can rise even though their rate is unchanged. That is expected, and shows where the next bottleneck appears.
